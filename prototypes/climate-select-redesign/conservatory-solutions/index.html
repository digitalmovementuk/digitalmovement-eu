Common problems

Why conservatories get too hot or too cold

Glass lets in light, but it also lets heat in and out. That's why most conservatories are unusable for much of the year.

Too hot in summer

Glazing lets in a lot of heat, so the room can become unusable on bright days.

Too cold in winter

Glass loses heat quickly, so the room is hard to use for much of the year.

Looks and planning

In a conservation area, or where appearance matters, changing the roof or adding external equipment may not be an option.

Your options

Why not replace the roof or insulate it?

Both common fixes change the room you already have. Here's how each option compares.

Option one

Replace the roof

You lose the glass roof, and with it the light in the conservatory and in the rooms next to it.

Option two

Insulate the roof

Covering the glass removes the light you came for, and the room still needs heating and cooling.

Questions

Conservatory questions, answered

Can you cool a conservatory with glass sides?

Yes. We have systems that can fit to glass sides, as well as glass roofs.

Do I need to replace my roof?

No. The system works with your existing roof and glass, so you keep the light and the look of the conservatory.

Will it heat the conservatory in winter?

Yes. Our systems provide heating in winter as well as cooling in summer.

Is it cheaper than replacing the roof?

Our systems are usually cheaper to install than replacing the roof or insulating. Your survey confirms the cost for your property before you order.

What if my home is in a conservation area?

Your surveyor reviews your property, including any visual impact concerns, before recommending a system.
